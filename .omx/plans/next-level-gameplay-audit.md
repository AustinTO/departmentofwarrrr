# Gameplay systems audit

Scope: read-only audit of the current working tree, including the uncommitted 3D pinball and weapon-wheel changes. Findings are grounded in source references; no tests or runtime changes were made.

## Current status — 2026-10-28

The findings below preserve the audit snapshot and must not be read as current status where noted. The live pinball kit now has three board shells with distinct upper-board bumper/scoop geometry; they share the protected shooter lane, dual ramps, and lower-playfield/flipper structure. This is board-layout diversity, not three fully distinct rule systems. The seeded RNG currently changes doctrine-card ordering and offsets the five-scenario fiscal-year cycle; it does not seed or alter pinball physics. Reassess the remaining progression/accounting issues against current source before implementation. Current verification reported for this snapshot: 129 tests across 16 files, geometry checks (33), and production build pass with a large-chunk warning; headless smoke stalled, and there is no physical-device or human-play evidence.

## Current loop

A run cycles through doctrine selection → 60-second (or manually ended) combat → theater readiness / inventory transfers → procurement pinball → mansion ledger → random event → production/readiness processing → press release → next doctrine. Combat and procurement both add taxpayer burn and contractor profit, and mansion thresholds turn accumulated profit into persistent unlocks. The structure has a strong satirical through-line and two distinct play modes, but several choices and progression rewards currently lack meaningful mechanical consequences.

## Prioritized findings

### P0 — Progression unlocks are disconnected from the live pinball game

`RunState` tracks `unlockedBoards` and `selectedBoard`, and saves/loads both ([RunState.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/state/RunState.ts:29), [RunState.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/state/RunState.ts:207)). `PinballBoards` defines three board IDs and unlock thresholds, but the definitions point to the same bumper set and `liveTable` geometry ([PinballBoards.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/PinballBoards.ts:585)). The active procurement scene constructs `Procurement3DGame` without a board ID ([ProcurementScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/ProcurementScene.ts:35)); the 3D game hardcodes its title, markup, and imports features from one `table.ts` ([Procurement3DGame.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/pinball3d/Procurement3DGame.ts:47), [table.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/pinball3d/table.ts:25)). No runtime use of `selectedBoard` or `PINBALL_BOARDS` appears in the 3D route. As a result, board unlocks and selection are cosmetic/dead progression; the unlocked Phaser/Planck board data does not affect the live game.

**Improvement:** Wire a board-selection step into readiness/procurement and pass the selected definition into the active machine/view/physics; make board definitions mechanically distinct (layout, targets, risk/reward) or remove the unlock track until they are.

**Acceptance criteria:** Each unlocked board can be selected; locked boards cannot; selected ID is honored by the live 3D rendering and collision/gameplay setup; each board has at least one verifiable mechanical distinction; selection survives reload; changing the board does not reset campaign state.

### P1 — Combat difficulty and wave pacing scale in ways that can overwhelm or undermine choice

`WaveDirector` increases intensity by 50% per campaign year (`fy` is `currentFY - 2025`) and also doubles it over the 90-second ramp, while the actual combat window is 60 seconds ([WaveDirector.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/WaveDirector.ts:15), [WaveDirector.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/WaveDirector.ts:27), [CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:99)). Spawn delay bottoms at 400ms, yielding up to 2.5 spawn events/sec; SWARM creates five simultaneous threats ([WaveDirector.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/WaveDirector.ts:52), [CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:1202)). Threat speed and damage do not appear to scale with FY, so pressure is mostly object-count and manual input burden. An immediate END YEAR control is available throughout the wave ([CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:207)); ending early avoids risk while still banking all current earnings, with no explicit time-based penalty.

**Improvement:** Calibrate spawn budget against FY and elapsed time; signal escalation and distinguish swarm groups; define an explicit early-exit tradeoff (e.g. unspawned threats do not count as survived/grade) or remove/confirm end-year as an intentional yield action.

**Acceptance criteria:** Simulated/recorded spawn counts and base damage remain within a target band for early, mid, and late campaign years; no spawn burst exceeds designed on-screen capacity; end-year behavior is clearly communicated and cannot yield a better grade/profit outcome than completing the same wave without a deliberate reason.

### P1 — Weapon economics make “aim and fire” mostly a cost decision, not a tactical one

Starter arsenal includes cheap auto-firing Vulcan, a large-area jammer, standard SM-3, and Block II from the outset ([config.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/config.ts:53), [config.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/config.ts:151)). Vulcan auto-fires at the highest threat in range and excludes allies ([CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:1328)); this makes it a low-cost default that can reduce manual weapon choice to occasional area effects. Jammer affects every threat in a 350px radius and then destroys affected targets after a short delay ([config.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/config.ts:66), [CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:709)). Weapon `range` is declared in config but manual targeting does not enforce it; weapons instead have bespoke shapes/effects, and interceptor target points need not correspond to enemies ([CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:604)). Ammo consumption is waived whenever a doctrine provides `ammoCapacity`, but the `999` value is treated as unlimited and not decremented ([CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:615), [CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:1250)). The UI does communicate unlimited doctrine ammo, though wheel labels still show the underlying inventory ([CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:497)).

**Improvement:** Give every weapon a clear niche with a readable matchup/targeting rule, establish whether range is a real constraint, and ensure wheel/callout ammo labels agree with active doctrine behavior. Tune auto-fire cadence and jammer coverage to preserve meaningful manual decisions.

**Acceptance criteria:** Each weapon has at least one encounter where it is the efficient/appropriate choice; Vulcan alone cannot trivially clear routine waves; range behavior is consistent with UI/config; doctrine ammo is shown consistently and has a documented gameplay effect; limited-ammo weapons cannot be fired with zero stock.

### P1 — Friendly-fire grading is binary and encourages passive play

Allied contacts are forced every 16 spawn selections (or probabilistically from 10), but friendly contacts that survive to the bottom are automatically spared and grant a fixed $18M bonus ([WaveDirector.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/WaveDirector.ts:34), [CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:1307), [CombatSystem.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/CombatSystem.ts:76)). Shooting one removes 12 readiness, increments pressure and changes grade; shooting at all is possible with any blast radius, and combat hit resolution routes friendly contacts to the scandal handler ([CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:1018), [CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:1046)). Grades are determined almost entirely by friendly-hit count and a 10-kill combo threshold; ordinary threat kills/readiness losses barely affect grade ([CombatSystem.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/CombatSystem.ts:92)). Since pass-through is always safe, restraint is often “do nothing,” while weapons with broad/automatic damage can randomly trigger a severe penalty.

**Improvement:** Make allies readable early and create a positive tactical restraint decision (e.g. crossing a protected lane or timing a shot), and grade across survival/readiness, restraint, efficiency, and chain rather than only a binary incident gate.

**Acceptance criteria:** Ally status is identifiable at small sizes and before a shot resolves; safe play rewards active timing/positioning, not just inaction; grade breakdown identifies how each major objective contributed; an accidental single friendly hit does not collapse an otherwise exceptional run without a clear, intentional severity rule.

### P1 — Procurement score inflation has weak strategic consequences

Procurement bumper hits immediately add taxpayer burn and contractor profit at 15% margin; positive contracts can stack with combo multipliers up to 4x ([ProcurementSystem.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/ProcurementSystem.ts:38), [ProcurementSystem.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/ProcurementSystem.ts:42)). Each hit can add weapon inventory to a contract and `authorize()` queues those batches, but the 3D game maps most hits to an interceptor item and the production system sends delivered units to reserve, not active theater ([Procurement3DGame.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/pinball3d/Procurement3DGame.ts:450), [ProcurementSystem.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/ProcurementSystem.ts:107), [ProductionSystem.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/ProductionSystem.ts:35)). Delivery lead time is calculated from summed table delay, but the player can authorize at any time and the delay mostly affects the fiscal-year integer rather than combat availability in the immediate next wave. `multiballTriggered` is set by one legacy bumper ID, but the live 3D integration’s emergency target ID is different, so its advertised multiball effect is not connected ([ProcurementSystem.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/ProcurementSystem.ts:60), [Procurement3DGame.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/pinball3d/Procurement3DGame.ts:394)).

**Improvement:** Make procurement outcomes feed a visible, useful next-year choice: which weapon contract to fund, delivery date, active vs reserve allocation, and a risk/cost/readiness consequence. Tie labels and mechanics to the same IDs; make multiball either work or remove its promise.

**Acceptance criteria:** Every contract’s weapon type, quantity, cost, delivery date, and destination are visible before authorization; at least one subsequent combat decision is materially changed by contract delivery; emergency funding behavior matches its text; no reward silently disappears into an inaccessible stockpile.

### P2 — Run progression has no meaningful tactical loadout planning or board use

RunState persists theater inventory and production queue and provides transfers, but readiness UI only offers fixed batches of up to 10 and does not show post-transfer inventory until restart ([ReadinessScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/ReadinessScene.ts:75), [ReadinessScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/ReadinessScene.ts:104)). Only transfer-from has a readiness penalty; transfers are free into active and reserve transfers are exempt ([RunState.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/state/RunState.ts:185)). Active combat ammo becomes a tactical constraint in later years, while doctrine can remove it; the interface shows inventory but does not forecast likely next-year weapon demand. Board unlocks currently have no gameplay integration (P0).

**Improvement:** Turn readiness into a compact planning phase with transfer quantities, actual risk/cost tradeoffs and a view of queued delivery; surface board/weapon unlock affordances where they are usable.

**Acceptance criteria:** Player can choose transfer quantity and see resulting stock/readiness before confirming; transfers have understandable rules in both directions; queue deliveries and active/reserve stock are distinguishable; every unlocked progression reward has a reachable use.

### P2 — Randomness is not seeded or surfaced, making balance/replay learning difficult

Wave composition, scenario threat bias, doctrine cards and events all use `Math.random()` independently ([WaveDirector.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/WaveDirector.ts:32), [CombatScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/CombatScene.ts:321), [DoctrineScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/DoctrineScene.ts:33), [EventScene.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/scenes/EventScene.ts:80)). There is no run seed/summary or persistent per-run challenge variation. This makes replay comparisons and reproducible balance tuning hard; event choice is a single random effect with no player decision.

**Improvement:** Introduce a run seed and include it in campaign summary/leaderboard; vary scenarios/events with understandable player choices and enough deterministic structure to learn enemy patterns.

**Acceptance criteria:** A displayed run seed reproduces combat/event selection; leaderboard entries include the seed and useful performance dimensions; event cards present at least two tradeoff choices rather than one compulsory random outcome.

### P2 — The 3D pinball loop has weak post-run closure and only one run format

The 3D machine starts with three balls and allows manual contract authorization as soon as positive value exists; after draining the last ball, it prompts authorization/return, while authorization immediately exits to Mansion ([Procurement3DGame.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/pinball3d/Procurement3DGame.ts:34), [Procurement3DGame.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/pinball3d/Procurement3DGame.ts:302), [Procurement3DGame.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/pinball3d/Procurement3DGame.ts:341)). There is no score/history persistence for pinball performance, and board variants do not change the active table. A one-shot `ProcurementSystem` resets per game object; exiting without authorizing loses that table’s uncommitted contract while campaign burn/profit may already have been incremented bumper-by-bumper ([Procurement3DGame.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/pinball3d/Procurement3DGame.ts:22), [ProcurementSystem.ts](/home/austin/Documents/trae_projects/DepartmentofWarrrr/src/game/ProcurementSystem.ts:57)). This creates unclear “quit vs authorize” semantics.

**Improvement:** Clarify contract settlement and persist a per-year procurement result; offer a concise summary at the end (value, items, delay, bonuses, final balance), and add board/ruleset variation once selected boards are wired.

**Acceptance criteria:** Leaving a table cannot retain burn/profit while discarding the associated uncommitted contract without an explicit warning/choice; one procurement result is recorded per fiscal year; the ending summary reconciles the campaign ledger; a new campaign year changes a real board objective or challenge.

## Suggested implementation order

1. Wire or retire the board-unlock system (P0); it is currently a broken progression promise.
2. Resolve procurement accounting/settlement and make rewards affect next combat (P1).
3. Tune wave pacing, early end-year semantics, and weapon niches together (P1), since these interact with campaign balance.
4. Improve friendly-contact grading/readability (P1).
5. Add transfer planning and deterministic/replayable campaign variation (P2).
